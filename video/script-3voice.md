# Disha Buddy — 3-voice narration script

For the 112 s film (30 fps). Three teammates, one section each, recorded separately.

**Rules we're writing to:**
- Real human voices only. No TTS.
- Open on a hook, not a label. The first line is a student's real question, not "this is our product."
- Never state a limitation as a limitation. Every design decision is stated as a decision.
  We do not say "only", "just", "simple", "never full chats", "we don't promise".
- Plain speech. Say the number, land the line, stop.

---

## Who says what

| Part | Speaker | Time | Length | Covers |
|------|---------|------|--------|--------|
| 1 | **Person A** | 0:00 – 0:49 | 49 s | Hook · Problem · Solution |
| 2 | **Person B** | 0:49 – 1:15 | **26 s (shortest)** | The demo |
| 3 | **Person C** | 1:15 – 1:44 | 29 s | How it's built · Close |
| 4 | **All three, filmed separately** | 1:44 – 1:52 | 8 s | Team clip |

---

## Part 1 — Person A (0:00 – 0:49)

**Tone:** you are quoting a real kid, then telling the judges why nobody answered them. Level, unhurried, a little cold. Do not sell yet.

### 0:00 – 0:10 · The hook

> "Bhaiya, seventy-eight percent aaye. Ab kya karun?"
>
> *(beat)*
>
> Fifteen lakh Indian students ask that question every year.
> Almost none of them get an answer.

*(23 words. Say the Hinglish line the way a nervous 17-year-old would text it, not
as a narrator reading a quote. Then stop for a full beat before the statistic.
That silence is the hook.)*

### 0:10 – 0:29 · Why nobody answers

> Ninety-three percent of Indian schools have no career counsellor.
> Where there is one, they carry three thousand students.
> India needs about fourteen lakh counsellors. It has under ten thousand.
> So the students who need guidance most, first-generation, small-town, no money
> for private counselling, get none of it.

*(46 words. Four flat facts, no adjectives. Slow down on "under ten thousand"
and let the last sentence land heavier than the rest.)*

### 0:29 – 0:49 · What we built

> So we built Disha.
> It lives on Telegram, talks like students talk, and remembers what matters:
> marks, budget, stream, every deadline.
> It names colleges that fit, with the source and date next to every number.
> And it doesn't wait to be asked. Before a round closes, Disha texts you first.

*(48 words. "Disha texts you first" is the sentence the whole film is built on.
Small pause before it, then say it plainly and stop. No lift at the end.)*

---

## Part 2 — Person B (0:49 – 1:15)

**Tone:** narrating something that is happening, not describing a feature. Shortest
part. The screen is doing the work, so stay out of its way.

### 0:49 – 1:15 · The demo

> Riya types one line. Seventy-eight percent, budget one lakh, UP.
> Seconds later she has colleges she can actually get into, with fees, cutoffs,
> and a link to where each number came from.
>
> Two days later, Disha messages her first:
> "Riya, AKTU round two kal band ho raha hai."
>
> Nobody asked it to. That is the whole product.

*(57 words, the densest block. Two rules: say the two Hinglish lines the way
people actually say them, and leave a real gap before "Two days later" so the
audience feels the time pass. "That is the whole product" is said quietly.)*

---

## Part 3 — Person C (1:15 – 1:44)

**Tone:** the engineer, stating decisions. Confident and specific. Nothing here is
hedged or apologised for.

### 1:15 – 1:32 · How it's built

> Disha is an agent, not a chatbot: memory for every student, answers pulled from
> a college dataset we crawled ourselves, and "let me check" instead of an
> invented cutoff.
> We store one line per student, never the conversation.
> And it runs GPU-free on Intel's OpenVINO.

*(44 words in 17 s, the fastest block. Keep a steady clip through the list.
"never the conversation" is a boast, not a disclaimer. Say it like one.)*

### 1:32 – 1:44 · Close

> Next: every state, more languages, WhatsApp, scholarships.
> A counsellor can hold thirty students. Software can hold thirty lakh.
>
> *(pause ~1 s)*
>
> Disha Buddy. Sahi disha, sahi decision.

*(24 words. The thirty / thirty-lakh line is the closer. Hit both numbers evenly,
then take the full pause before the tagline. Do not rush the last four words.)*

---

## Part 4 — Team clip (1:44 – 1:52)

We're all remote, so **nobody films together.** Each person records a solo clip on
their own phone and we cut them into one 8-second sequence in the edit.

**Each person films ~4 seconds** (say your line, then hold still, still looking at
the lens, for 2 more seconds so the editor has room to cut).

> **A:** "I'm ____." <br>
> **B:** "I'm ____." <br>
> **C:** "I'm ____, and we built Disha Buddy."

Cut sequentially: A at 1:44, B at 1:46.5, C at 1:49, holding to 1:52. Straight cuts,
no transitions.

**So the three clips match:**
- Film **horizontal (landscape)**, 1080p, phone propped up, not handheld.
- Face a **window or a lamp**. Light in front of you, nothing bright behind your head.
- Plain wall behind you. Frame from chest up, eyes about a third down the frame.
- Something plain and non-striped, no logos.
- Record a **quiet 3-second clip of your room** before speaking, so the editor can
  noise-match the three of us.

If you say it in Hindi or Hinglish, burn English subtitles into that clip.

**Fallback if the cuts look mismatched:** same three clips as a 3-panel split screen,
vertical thirds, everyone on screen while each says their line. No re-shoot needed.

## What changed from the first draft, and why

Keep these rules if you rewrite any line.

| Cut | Reason |
|---|---|
| "This is Disha Buddy, a free mentor on Telegram" as the opening | A label is not a hook. Judges watch dozens of these. Open on the student's question and let them feel the gap before we name ourselves. |
| "with cited sources, **never false promises**" | Argues against ourselves. Nobody accused us of lying. "Source and date next to every number" says the same thing as a strength. |
| "a **simple** CRM tracks students" | Never call your own work simple. |
| "**one-line notes only, never full chats**" said apologetically | Same fact, moved to the engineering section and stated as a decision we are proud of: "We store one line per student, never the conversation." |
| "and can't afford private guidance" trailing off | Weak ending to the problem. Replaced with a landing line: the students who need it most get none of it. |
| "Here's what it looks like" | Filler. The demo has already started. |
| "So every student has someone to ask" | Vague. Replaced with the counsellor-versus-software contrast, which is a real argument. |

## Recording notes

Three different rooms, so consistency is the thing to watch. Mismatched room tone is
what makes a stitched voiceover sound stitched.

- Record each part as its **own file**: `vo-a.wav`, `vo-b.wav`, `vo-c.wav`.
  Easier to redo one person than the whole thing.
- **Same mic type for all three.** All phone voice-memos, or all laptop mics. Do not
  mix a headset with a phone; the tone shift is audible on the cut.
- Mic 15 to 20 cm from your mouth, slightly off-axis so plosives don't pop. A small
  soft room (bed, curtains, clothes) beats a big empty one.
- Kill fans, AC, notifications. Record a **3-second silent clip** of your room first.
- Read your part **twice** in one take with a 3-second gap. Pick the better one later.
- Leave ~0.5 s of silence at the head and tail of each file.
- Send raw files. Do not re-export through WhatsApp, it crushes the audio.

## Putting it together

1. Drop the **silent** master on the timeline (`out/disha-buddy.mp4`).
2. Lay `vo-a` at 0:00, `vo-b` at 0:49, `vo-c` at 1:15. Nudge each until the spoken
   line matches the caption on screen.
3. Splice the three team clips over 1:44 to 1:52.
4. Export H.264 MP4. Under 120 s and under 60 MB.

The on-screen captions still carry the **old** wording. They get rewritten to this
script in `src/timings.ts` as part of the video redesign, then re-rendered.
